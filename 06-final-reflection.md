# Final Reflection

> "A computer does not understand the real world directly. It works with representations of information and follows instructions for processing those representations."

A computer doesn't understand things the way humans do. It doesn't know what "breakfast" or "money" or "Victor" means. People turn real things into data (text, numbers, true/false), and the computer follows exact instructions on that data. If the representation is wrong or the instructions are unclear, the result is wrong.

- **Breakfast:** I had to turn a vague idea into exact steps.
- **ATM:** the real act of withdrawing cash became an ordered procedure with failure handling.
- **Calculator:** numbers go in, an operation is performed, and a result comes out.
- **Customer data:** a person's details became text, numbers, and true/false values.
- **Online store:** an order became data that gets processed, stored, and answered with a confirmation.
